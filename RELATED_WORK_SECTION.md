# Related Work

Aligned language models can produce harmful continuations when their response begins with an adversarial prefix ([Andriushchenko et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02151); [Wang et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.219/)). Mechanistic accounts connect these failures to autoregressive consistency and early refusal computations ([Lyu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04168); [Kwon, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14147)). Weak-to-strong jailbreaking similarly uses smaller unsafe models to elicit harmful outputs from stronger aligned models ([Zhao et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17256)). These studies establish inference-time elicitation. We investigate whether student-generated prefixes turn a frozen teacher's continuation supervision into persistent amplification of student harmfulness or bias, despite safe responses when the teacher generates from scratch.

On-policy distillation queries teachers on student-generated trajectories ([Agarwal et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13649)), but those prefixes can distort supervision ([Zhu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11182)). [Xie et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22600) address accumulated teacher–student divergence through importance weighting, while [Armandpour et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10889) diagnose supervision through per-token gradient alignment with task success. Safety-oriented OPD instead improves targets through privileged safety context (OPSA; [Fu et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15239)) or activation steering and localized updates (SafeSteer; [Li et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02530)). Our focus is whether existing student tendencies compromise otherwise safe teacher supervision, causing OPD to amplify harmfulness or bias.

Context distillation already internalizes context-dependent behavior ([Snell et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.15189)), and both fine-tuning and distillation of ostensibly safe refusals can degrade safety ([Qi et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03693); [Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11157)). More directly, [Askin et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12798) compare behavioral transfer across supervised fine-tuning, off-policy distillation, and OPD, finding that aligned teachers can realign misaligned students. We investigate a boundary condition on this corrective effect: student-generated prefixes that compromise the teacher's continuation policy and amplify pre-existing harmfulness or bias. The central distinction is between supervision on student-generated trajectories and ordinary teacher-generated demonstrations.
